Blog Writing Guidelines

Two things to follow for every Alphabyte blog post.

A pre-publish checklist and a default post structure. The current blog reads like internal documentation, with long unbroken paragraphs, no internal links, no cited statistics, and no hooks. The two blocks below are everything needed to fix that.

/ 01 Pre-publish checklist

No post ships until every box is checked. The check takes about ten minutes and is what prevents the patterns this guide exists to kill from creeping back in.

  • Hook in the first two sentences. Direct answer in the first paragraph. No definition openers, no history lessons.
  • At least three external citations to named, reputable sources (Anthropic, Gartner, Forrester, McKinsey, Deloitte, Statista, official Canadian government data, Search Engine Land, peer-reviewed papers). Each is linked and named in the body text.
  • At least five internal links to Alphabyte service, tool, case study, blog, or team pages. Anchor text is the descriptive keyword, never "click here".
  • FAQ section at the bottom with three to five real questions (pulled from discovery calls, "People Also Ask", and the inbox). FAQPage JSON-LD shipped on the page.
  • Title 50 to 55 characters. Meta description 140 to 155 characters. Primary keyword in the first 40 of the title and first 90 of the meta. Slug is clean: three to six lowercase hyphenated words, primary keyword first.
  • No paragraph over four sentences. Most paragraphs are two or three. White space is what makes long-form readable on a phone and gives an LLM a clean unit to extract.
  • Every H2 and H3 is a question or a complete-sentence position. No "Background", "Context", or "Conclusion" labels.
  • Each section answers its heading directly in the first one to two sentences. Reasoning, nuance, and examples come after.
  • At least one specific "what we have seen" passage drawing on real Alphabyte engagement experience. This is the E-E-A-T signal Google rewards and the kind of original passage AI engines preferentially cite.
  • No em dashes. Anywhere. Search the final draft for the character before publishing. Use periods, commas, colons, or parentheses instead.
  • Canadian English spelling throughout ("optimise", "organisation", "behaviour"). Numerals from 10 onward. Spell out one through nine.
  • Every image has descriptive alt text and a caption that adds a point rather than repeating the image. No stock photography. No people-in-offices, no glowing brains.

/ 02 Default post structure

Not every post follows this exactly, but most should. Use it as the scaffold for the first draft. The structure is engineered to maximise both human scan-readability and AI-engine extractability.

SectionWhat goes in itLength
H1 Descriptive, sentence-style. Includes the primary keyword. Can be longer than the title tag. One line
Hook Two sentences that frame the question and the friction. No definitions, no history. 2 sentences
Direct answer The thesis of the post in one paragraph. Three to four sentences. This is what AI engines extract. 1 paragraph
Why now One paragraph with one external citation. Why this matters in 2026 specifically. 1 to 2 paragraphs
Body H2 #1 First major section. Heading is a question or position. Lead with the answer. Cite once. 200 to 350 words
Body H2 #2 Second major section. Same rules. Include one "what we have seen" passage. 200 to 350 words
Body H2 #3 Third major section. Often a comparison or counter-argument. Link to a relevant case study. 200 to 350 words
Body H2 #4 (Optional) Fourth section. Use if the topic genuinely needs it. Do not pad. 200 to 350 words
FAQ Three to five real questions, each answered in one to three sentences. Wrapped in FAQPage schema. 250 to 450 words
Closing One short paragraph that ties back to the hook and points to a relevant Alphabyte service. Not a sales paragraph. 2 to 4 sentences
How to use this. Open the checklist in a second tab while editing. Match the draft to the structure table for the first three or four posts until the pattern becomes muscle memory. Target total length 1,200 to 2,200 words. Cadence two to four posts per month. Quality over volume: two posts that follow these rules will outperform six that do not.
